## The story of our model
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<div style="display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:.4em;max-width:900px;margin:0 auto;text-align:left;font-size:.56em;line-height:1.35">
<div><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">Question</strong>: what does a cost of moving do to where people work, to wages and to output?</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">Two sectors</strong>, farm $F$ and city $C$, and $L$ workers in all: $L_F + L_C = L$</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">Output</strong> in each sector:
<div style="display:flex;flex-direction:column;align-items:flex-start;gap:.15em;margin:.25em 0 .1em 1.5em">
<span>$Y_F = A_F\,L_F^{\gamma_F}$</span>
<span>$Y_C = A_C\,L_C^{\gamma_C}$</span>
<span style="opacity:.85">in short $Y_X = A_X\,L_X^{\gamma_X}$, with $X \in \{F, C\}$ and $0 &lt; \gamma_X &lt; 1$: on the same land, or in the same factory, each extra worker adds less</span>
</div></div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">What one more worker adds</strong>, the marginal product: $MPL_X = \gamma_X\,A_X\,L_X^{\gamma_X-1}$. It falls as $L_X$ grows, and rises with $A_X$</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">Wage and marginal product</strong>: in a market economy the wage equals the $MPL$. In a plan it need not, but total output still depends on the $MPL$. So in pre-market China the vertical axis of the two-origin diagram is the $MPL$, not the wage: read "wage" as $MPL$ throughout. The output lost, the cost of the inefficiency, is measured correctly either way</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">Mechanism</strong>: free to move, $MPL_F = MPL_C$ and output is as large as possible. With the hukou, $MPL_C - MPL_F = \tau$: too many on the farm, output lost</div>
</div>

## Does the hukou change?
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<ul>
<li>If $\tau$ never moved, we could not tell the barrier from everything else that makes cities richer</li>

## How to tell a paper
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<div style="display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:.35em;max-width:900px;margin:0 auto;text-align:left;font-size:.56em;line-height:1.3">
<div><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">1. Motivation</strong>: start from the <strong>title</strong>. What puzzle or fact makes the question worth asking?</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">2. Question</strong>: one sentence, generally with a cause and an effect</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">3. Data</strong>: what is measured, where, when. And: <strong>could the data be wrong?</strong> If they were, what would it do to the answer?</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">4. Empirical strategy</strong>: the comparison, and the <strong>rules of the world</strong> we must assume for it to give the causal effect</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">5. Results</strong>: the numbers, with their size in words</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">6. Robustness</strong>: are we sure the rules of the world hold in this case? How do the authors test alternative explanations, and possible biases, of what they find?</div>
<div class="fragment"><strong style="color:#ffcf5a">7. Why it matters</strong>: for China, for policy, for what we thought we knew</div>
</div>

## Question
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<p class="qkick">Tombe and Zhu (2019) · 3. Data, and what if they are wrong</p>
<p class="qtext">What if the census did not see all the migrants?</p>

<div class="fragment">
<ul>
<li>The cost of moving is not observed: it is read off <strong>how many people move</strong>, given the income gap. Fewer movers counted means a <strong>higher</strong> measured cost</li>
<li>Who could be missing: seasonal workers; people hiding from the police (before 2003 a migrant without a permit risked <strong>deportation</strong>); migrants who got the local hukou, counted as <strong>locals</strong></li>
<li>If the same share is missing in 2000 and in 2005, it matters little: the results use the <strong>change</strong>, and a constant bias cancels</li>
<li>If more people hid in 2000 than in 2005, the <strong>fall</strong> in the cost looks larger than it was. People missing from the data: <strong>sample selection</strong></li>
</ul>
</div>

## Empirical strategy
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<p class="qkick">Tombe and Zhu (2019) · 4. Empirical strategy</p>

<ul style="font-size:.62em">
<li>We cannot <strong>see</strong> the cost of moving. We read it from what people <strong>choose</strong>, as with revealed preferences in micro</li>
<li class="fragment">For every pair of places: how much more one earns <strong>there</strong> than <strong>here</strong>, and how many people <strong>actually move</strong></li>
<li class="fragment">A big gap and few movers: moving must be <strong>very costly</strong>. A small gap and many movers: it is cheap</li>
<li class="fragment">Part of the cost is real: a migrant <strong>loses the income</strong> of the village land. And people differ: some would move for little, some for no money at all</li>
<li class="fragment">Goods the same way: provinces that buy mostly <strong>from themselves</strong> face high costs of trading</li>
<li class="fragment">We compare <strong>2000 with 2005</strong>: what is fixed cancels</li>
<li class="fragment">What <strong>changes</strong> over time (highways, amenities) stays in the measured fall</li>
</ul>

---

## Rules of the world
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<p class="qkick">Tombe and Zhu (2019) · 4. Empirical strategy</p>
<ul>
<li>Each worker picks the province and sector with the highest real income <strong>net of the cost</strong>, with personal tastes on top</li>
<li class="fragment">The cost is paid <strong>every year</strong> one lives without the local hukou; migrants <strong>lose their share</strong> of village land income</li>
<li class="fragment">Provinces do not differ in how pleasant they are</li>
<li class="fragment">So everything else is counted as <strong>cost</strong>, including the attachment to one's land</li>
</ul>

## Question
<!-- .slide: class="dense" -->

<p class="qkick">Khanna, Liang, Mobarak and Song (2025) · 3. Data, and what if they are wrong</p>
<p class="qtext">Why not the official pollution data? And who could be missing from the census?</p>
<div class="fragment">
<ul>
<li>The authors: official air-quality data "may be subject to <strong>manipulation</strong> by local governments"</li>

## Additional material: the model's equation
<!-- .slide: class="dense" id="tz-equation" -->

<p class="qkick">Tombe and Zhu (2019), equation (10) and section III.A</p>

<div style="font-size:.6em;line-height:1.5;text-align:left;max-width:900px;margin:0 auto">
<p style="margin:.2em 0">Share of the workers registered in province $n$, sector $j$, who work in province $i$, sector $k$:</p>
<p style="text-align:center;margin:.3em 0">$m_{ni}^{jk} = \dfrac{\left(V_i^k\,\delta_{ni}^{jk}/\mu_{ni}^{jk}\right)^{\kappa}}{\sum_{k'}\sum_{i'}\left(V_{i'}^{k'}\,\delta_{ni'}^{jk'}/\mu_{ni'}^{jk'}\right)^{\kappa}}$</p>
<p class="fragment" style="margin:.4em 0">Compare movers with stayers and solve for the cost:</p>
<p class="fragment" style="text-align:center;margin:.3em 0">$\mu_{ni}^{jk} = \dfrac{1}{\delta_{nn}^{jj}}\cdot\dfrac{V_i^k}{V_n^j}\cdot\left(\dfrac{m_{nn}^{jj}}{m_{ni}^{jk}}\right)^{1/\kappa}$</p>
<ul class="fragment" style="font-size:.85em;margin:.4em 0 0">
<li>$V$: real income per worker in each place; $m$: who stays ($m_{nn}^{jj}$) and who moves ($m_{ni}^{jk}$), from the censuses</li>
<li>$\delta_{nn}^{jj}$: the village land income that stayers keep and migrants lose (about 1.9 on average)</li>
<li>$\kappa$: how much tastes for places differ across people, estimated at about 1.5</li>
<li>A big income ratio and few movers: a big $\mu$</li>
