How to read it

  • Each bar is a Home firm, the least productive on the left. Height = sales: blue at home, amber abroad. The thin outline is what the firm sold in autarky; grey dashed bars are firms that close. The strip below gives each firm's change in sales. Point at a bar, or click it, for its numbers.
  • Press Closed, then Open. Firms to the left of the survival cutoff close; firms to the right of the export cutoff export. Every firm loses the same share of its home sales; exporters more than make it up abroad.
  • No firm becomes more productive (until you turn learning on). Average productivity rises because the weakest leave and sales move to the best: selection and reallocation. Open "Where productivity growth comes from".
  • "Help to export" closes more firms. A lower export fixed cost lets more firms export; they hire more workers, the real wage rises, and the weakest firms can no longer cover their fixed cost.

The rules of this world

  • Two identical countries, Home and Foreign: the same wage and the same price index in both. One partner (n = 1).
  • Every firm charges the same markup over its marginal cost, σ/(σ−1) = 1.5 with σ = 3. Each firm's productivity φ is drawn once and never changes.
  • Producing needs a fixed cost fD; exporting needs another, fX, and the iceberg cost τ. Fixed costs make small firms unprofitable: a firm survives only if its sales cover σ fD = 3.
  • Why home sales fall: foreign firms now sell in Home, and exporters hire more workers, which raises the real wage. In Melitz (2003) exit is driven by this competition for workers; markups do not fall, because with this demand they are fixed.

Three mechanisms, kept apart

  • Scale (Krugman 1980): increasing returns and a bigger market. Firms are all alike, so all of them export and nobody is selected; the gain is more varieties for consumers.
  • Selection (Melitz 2003): firms differ. Trade moves the two cutoffs; the weakest exit, the best export, and average productivity rises with no firm changing.
  • Learning (De Loecker 2007): exporters themselves become more productive. Turn it on: the bar "survivors get better" in the decomposition becomes positive. Here only the exporters' sales are recomputed, with the cutoffs held fixed: an illustration, not a new equilibrium.

Limits

  • Identical countries: no terms-of-trade effects, no exchange rate, and trade balanced by construction (Home's imports mirror its exports).
  • Workers move freely and at once between firms; nobody is unemployed. The model compares equilibria: after a trade war, the weak firms that "come back" stand for new entrants, while in reality exit is not easily undone.

The 80 firms sit at the quantiles of the Pareto distribution; the autarky cutoff is φDA = 1, so all 80 produce in autarky. With Pareto the number of entrants does not depend on trade, so the same 80 firms are the pool in every state. The cutoffs, the real wage and λ (Home's spending on Home goods) come from the formulas; the counts of firms are those of the 80 firms. Average productivity: the sales-weighted mean of log φ of Home firms; the tile shows exp of its change minus 1, the decomposition shows it in log points (Melitz and Polanec 2015).
