SOE Lab one industry · 48 firms

Twelve state firms and thirty-six private firms share a fixed amount of capital and labour. The state gives its firms cheap credit and pushes them to keep workers. Inputs go to the wrong firms, marginal products spread out, and output is lost.

Reading the model

Yi = Ai Kiα Li1−α   ·   TFPQi = Ai   ·   TFPRi = PiAi
MRPLi ∝ PiYi/Li = (1+τL,i) w   ·   MRPKi ∝ PiYi/Ki = (1+τK,i) r   ·   TFPRi ∝ (1+τK,i)α(1+τL,i)1−α
  • Without wedges, every firm has the same MRPL, MRPK and TFPR. The more productive firms are simply bigger: a higher TFPQ means more output, a lower price, and the same revenue per unit of input as everybody else.
  • TFPQ differences are technology; TFPR differences are the wedges. A wedge changes how much a firm uses and sells, and so its price, not what it can make with each unit of input. Press "Reallocate": the TFPQ row does not move, the other three collapse onto one line.
  • Errors in the data look like wedges. Open "Errors in the data": the true loss stays the same, the measured one rises. Press "Reallocate" with errors on: no wedge is left, yet the economist still measures a loss.
  • Cheap credit lowers the state firms' MRPK; extra workers lower their MRPL. They hold far more capital than their productivity justifies, and the good private firms are too small.
  • Making a favoured firm more productive is not a free lunch. It also pulls capital and workers away from firms where they produce more. In Hsieh and Song's data, with a fixed capital stock, this drag can exceed the gain (2015, Table 8).

Model of Hsieh and Klenow (2009) with the labour and capital wedges of Hsieh and Song (2015): one industry, elasticity of substitution σ = 3, capital share α = 1/3, total capital and labour fixed. Firm productivities are drawn once. The 1998 and 2007 presets set the two state-firm wedges so that state firms' output per worker and per unit of capital, relative to private firms, match Hsieh and Song's figures: about 60% and 35% in 1998, 75% and 47% in 2007 (BPEA 2015, pp. 310 and 314).
