Part 1 · A paper you have not seen

Made in China 2025: what did the money buy?

Guangwei Li and Lee G. Branstetter, "Does 'Made in China 2025' Work for China? Evidence from Chinese Listed Firms", Research Policy 53(6), 2024, article 105009. Numbers from the 2022 working-paper version (NBER Working Paper 30676).

In May 2015 China's State Council launched "Made in China 2025", a plan to turn the "world's factory" into an innovator in ten industries, from robots and aircraft to electric cars and medicines. Abroad, the plan caused alarm: in 2018 the United States cited it when it began its trade war with China. But did it work for China itself?

Lee Branstetter (Carnegie Mellon) and Guangwei Li (ShanghaiTech) ask whether the firms the plan supported received more money, spent more on research, and became more inventive, productive and profitable.

The first problem: the government never said which firms were in the plan. Listed companies must report every subsidy they receive, but of about 286,000 subsidy records only 109 name the plan. So the authors had a computer read every annual report of China's listed firms from 2015 to 2018, looking for the words "Made in China 2025". 1,120 of 3,486 firms used them, and a manual check showed that these firms expected to benefit. To apply for the plan's funds, a firm had to send three years of audited accounts and proof of its "technical level", including its intellectual property.

The authors then follow 1,657 listed manufacturing firms from 2011 to 2018. 718 of them are "targeted": they mention the plan. For each outcome, they compare how the targeted firms changed after they first mentioned the plan with how the other firms changed over the same years. Subsidies come from the firms' reports, patents (in China and in the United States) from a global patent database, productivity from sales and inputs.

Targeted firms received about a fifth more innovation subsidies, and raised research spending by 0.45 percentage points of sales, in a sample where the average is 3.9%. Their patents, productivity and profits did not change in any clear way. The data stop in 2018; from that year, with the trade war, the government spoke of the plan much less.
