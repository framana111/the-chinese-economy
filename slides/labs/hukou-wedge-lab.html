Hukou Wedge Lab

Two sectors, a fixed number of workers, and a cost τ that a worker pays to live and work in the city. Raise it and workers stay on the farm, wages diverge and output is lost. Lower it and watch the allocation recover.

0% means workers move freely. At 100%, the move costs as much as the wage a worker earns at the free-mobility optimum.

Reading the model

  • A wage gap is not a welfare gap. The marginal worker is indifferent: the urban wage exceeds the rural one by exactly τ, and τ is what moving costs.
  • τ is a cost, not a tax. Nobody collects it, so the output the held-back workers would have produced is simply lost: the shaded triangle in the diagram.
  • Output is lost at the margin. The first workers held back lose little; each additional one costs more because the urban marginal product keeps rising as the city empties.
