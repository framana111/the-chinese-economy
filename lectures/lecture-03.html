Lecture 3 — How do economists estimate causal effects? From Mao to the reforms

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Wednesday 28 October 2026 · Block: causality

Questions

  • What can we say about the effect of China's transition, and what can we not?
  • How do matching, regression and difference in differences close the "back door" of a causal graph, and what does each assume?
  • Did the years in the countryside change the lives of the sent-down youths? Did the land reform of 1978-84 change who was born, and did it raise yields?

Key concepts

Parallel trends · matching and comparing siblings · difference in differences and event studies · staggered rollout · "nature's lottery" and the logic of an instrument · holding many things fixed: regression and the propensity score · mean reversion · official data and satellite data · identification assumptions.

Readings

Required

  • Douglas Almond, Hongbin Li and Shuang Zhang, "Land Reform and Sex Selection in China", Journal of Political Economy 127(2), 2019, pp. 560-585 — introduction and sections 3-4 (working-paper version: NBER Working Paper 19153).

Optional (works cited on the slides)

  • Joel Ferguson and Oliver Kim, "What Started China's Miracle? The View from Outer Space", working paper, March 2026.
  • Yu Xie, Yang Jiang and Emily Greenman, "Did Send-down Experience Benefit Youth? A Reevaluation of the Social Consequences of Forced Urban-Rural Migration during China's Cultural Revolution", Social Science Research 37(2), 2008, pp. 686-700.
  • Justin Yifu Lin, "Rural Reforms and Agricultural Growth in China", American Economic Review 82(1), 1992, pp. 34-51.
  • Barry Naughton, The Chinese Economy: Transitions and Growth (MIT Press, 2007), pp. 59-62 and 72-76 (1961-1978), p. 89 (the Third Plenum).
  • Yi Chen, Ziying Fan, Xiaomin Gu and Li-An Zhou, "Arrival of Young Talent: The Send-Down Movement and Rural Education in China", American Economic Review 110(11), 2020, pp. 3393-3430.
  • Ludwig Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921; English translation by C. K. Ogden, 1922), proposition 7.

Suggested, on method (not required)

  • Nick Huntington-Klein, The Effect: An Introduction to Research Design and Causality (Chapman & Hall, 2025), free online at theeffectbook.net — Part 1, chapters 5-8 (identification, causal diagrams, closing back doors), with causal graphs and almost no mathematics; Part 2, chapters 13-18, for regression, matching, event studies and difference in differences.
  • An alternative: Scott Cunningham, Causal Inference: The Mixtape, free online at mixtape.scunning.com — chapter 3, "Directed Acyclic Graphs".
