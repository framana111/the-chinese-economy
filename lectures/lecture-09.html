Lecture 9 — Who pays? Value chains, surpluses and trade wars

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Wednesday 18 November 2026 · Block: trade

Questions

  • How Chinese is a product that says "Made in China"?
  • Where does the money go in a global value chain, and how has China moved up?
  • Why does China run a trade surplus, and what do Melitz and the macro story each explain?
  • What happened in the trade wars between the United States and China, from 2002 to 2026?
  • When the United States taxed foreign goods in 2018, who paid: the foreign seller or the American buyer?
  • Who gained and who lost inside America, and does the map explain why these tariffs were chosen?

Key concepts

Value chains · processing trade · domestic value added in exports · the smile curve · moving up and moving out · the current account · saving and consumption · the real exchange rate · pass-through · incidence of a tariff · unit values · hit and non-hit varieties · event study · complete pass-through · losses of buyers, gains of producers, tariff revenue · retaliation · tariffs and the electoral map.

In class

  1. Your objects, aloud: the homework of lecture 8.
  2. Value chains: who makes the money, and how China moved up.
  3. The macro side: a surplus, low consumption, the renminbi, and what Melitz does not see.
  4. Trade wars, 2002-2026.
  5. Fajgelbaum, Goldberg, Kennedy and Khandelwal (2020), told in the seven steps.
  6. Yeast or mushrooms? And next time.

Readings

On AulaWeb, enrolled students find the papers listed here, one zip file per lecture. Other books and a few older papers are not there, and some papers are there in their working-paper version.

Required

  • Pablo D. Fajgelbaum, Pinelopi K. Goldberg, Patrick J. Kennedy and Amit K. Khandelwal, "The Return to Protectionism", Quarterly Journal of Economics 135(1), 2020, pp. 1-55 — the introduction (Section I), the event study (Section II.D), and the political economy of the tariffs (Section V.F, "Tariff Protection, Wages, and Voting Patterns").

Optional

  • Hiau Looi Kee and Heiwai Tang, "Domestic Value Added in Exports: Theory and Firm Evidence from China", American Economic Review 106(6), 2016, pp. 1402-1436: how much of China's exports is made in China.
  • Yuqing Xing and Neal Detert, "How the iPhone Widens the United States Trade Deficit with the People's Republic of China", ADBI Working Paper 257, 2010 (revised 2011): the iPhone, part by part.
