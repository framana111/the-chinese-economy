Lecture 5 — From the hukou to the Lewis turning point

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Wednesday 4 November 2026 · Block: reallocation across sectors

Questions

  • What does our two-sector model assume, and what happens to its story if one of its rules fails?
  • How did the hukou change after 1980: who was allowed to move, and who to settle?
  • How large was the cost of moving inside China, how much did it fall, and how much growth did that bring?
  • Did the relaxation treat everyone alike? Who leaves polluted cities, and who may settle in the big ones?
  • Has China run out of farm workers?

Key concepts

Production function, Y = A·L<sup>γ</sup> in each sector · marginal product and wage · rules of the world (assumptions) · moving versus settling · the blue-stamp hukou · points systems · the cost of moving as a measured wedge · growth accounting · confounders and sample selection in data · instruments as nature's lotteries · skilled and unskilled workers · the Lewis turning point.

In class

  1. Homework with the Hukou Wedge Lab.
  2. The story of our model: question, rules of the world, mechanism, implications, and how to check them.
  3. The relaxation, 1980-2016.
  4. How to tell a paper in seven steps: motivation, question, data (and what if they are wrong), empirical strategy (and its rules of the world), results, robustness, why it matters.
  5. Tombe and Zhu (2019), then Khanna, Liang, Mobarak and Song (2025), told in the seven steps.
  6. Has China run out of farm workers? A debate.

Readings

Required

  • Trevor Tombe and Xiaodong Zhu, "Trade, Migration, and Productivity: A Quantitative Analysis of China", American Economic Review 109(5), 2019, pp. 1843-1872 — the introduction, the section on migration policies and patterns, and the results (skip the model's equations).
  • Barry Naughton, The Chinese Economy: Transitions and Growth (MIT Press, 2007), chapter 5, section 5.1.5, "Invisible Walls: Administrative Barriers Today" (pp. 124-126). Free to borrow (one-hour or two-week loan, free account) on the Internet Archive; the chapters required for this course will soon also be available to enrolled students on AulaWeb.

Optional (works cited on the slides)

  • Gaurav Khanna, Wenquan Liang, Ahmed Mushfiq Mobarak and Ran Song, "The Productivity Consequences of Pollution-Induced Migration in China", American Economic Journal: Applied Economics 17(2), 2025, pp. 184-224 — introduction and section 5.
  • Kam Wing Chan and Will Buckingham, "Is China Abolishing the Hukou System?", China Quarterly 195, 2008, pp. 582-606.
  • Xiaobo Zhang, Jin Yang and Shenglin Wang, "China has reached the Lewis turning point", China Economic Review 22(4), 2011, pp. 542-554.
  • Jane Golley and Xin Meng, "Has China run out of surplus labour?", China Economic Review 22(4), 2011, pp. 555-572.
  • Mitali Das and Papa N'Diaye, "Chronicle of a Decline Foretold: Has China Reached the Lewis Turning Point?", IMF Working Paper 13/26, 2013.
