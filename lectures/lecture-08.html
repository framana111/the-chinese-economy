Lecture 8 — Opening China

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Tuesday 17 November 2026 · Block: trade

Questions

  • Did China grow by perspiration or by inspiration: more inputs, or more output from the same inputs?
  • How did a closed economy open, between 1950 and its entry into the WTO?
  • Why are exporters a minority of firms, and why are they bigger and more productive?
  • Can a country become more productive when trade opens, even if no firm gets better?
  • What did the WTO tariff cuts do to the markups and the productivity of Chinese firms?
  • Did exporting make firms better, or were the better firms the ones that exported?

Key concepts

Perspiration and inspiration · creative accounting and creative destruction · processing trade · the WTO accession · fixed cost of producing and of exporting · iceberg trade cost · two thresholds: survive and export · selection · scale and variety · learning by exporting · markups · output tariffs and input tariffs · incumbents, entrants and exiters · difference in differences · a corrected result.

Required

  • Loren Brandt, Johannes Van Biesebroeck, Luhang Wang and Yifan Zhang, "WTO Accession and Performance of Chinese Manufacturing Firms", American Economic Review 107(9), 2017, pp. 2784-2820, with its corrigendum, American Economic Review 109(4), 2019, pp. 1616-1621 — the introduction; Section I on the policy regime and the tariff data (with Figure 3); and the main results (Section IV, on markups, productivity and the decomposition). Read the corrigendum too: the authors found a programming error in their own price index, and it changes one of the results.

Optional

  • Loren Brandt, Johannes Van Biesebroeck and Yifan Zhang, "Creative Accounting or Creative Destruction? Firm-Level Productivity Growth in Chinese Manufacturing", Journal of Development Economics 97(2), 2012, pp. 339-351.
  • Marc J. Melitz (2003): the model behind the lab.
  • Justin R. Pierce and Peter K. Schott, "The Surprisingly Swift Decline of US Manufacturing Employment", American Economic Review 106(7), 2016, pp. 1632-1662: the other side of the ocean.

The Melitz Lab

Open the Melitz Lab: two identical countries, eighty firms each, all different in productivity. Press "Closed", then "Open", then "Join the WTO: tariffs fall", and watch which firms close, which start exporting and which sell less at home. Then try "Trade war", and "Help to export" (a lower fixed cost of exporting), which closes more firms, not fewer. Change the trade cost and the fixed cost of exporting, switch on learning by exporting, and open the panel "Where productivity growth comes from" to see whether average productivity rises because firms get better or because the mix of firms changes. The lab's notes say what the model leaves out: no exchange rate, no terms of trade, and trade balanced by construction.
