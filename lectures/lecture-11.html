Lecture 11 — Innovation: who gains when you invent? A ray of hope from China's solar

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Tuesday 24 November 2026, online on Teams; it continues on Wednesday 25 November · Block: innovation

Questions

  • Why does innovation create externalities, and how many kinds of externality are there?
  • Where is all that Chinese R&D going? Which firms do research, and how much of growth comes from copying?
  • What if China had fewer distortions, or gave more subsidies to research?
  • Chinese cities paid for solar power in three ways: helping buyers, producers or inventors. Which one worked best?
  • Did China as a whole gain, and was it worth the money, for China and for the planet?

Key concepts

Externality · knowledge spillover · business stealing · duplication · learning by doing · demand spillover · clusters and crowding · pollution and carbon · through prices or around them · imitation and innovation · wedges · synthetic difference in differences · cut-offs (Melitz again) · general equilibrium · costs and benefits of a subsidy.

In class

  1. The end of lecture 10: the results of Chen, Liu, Suárez Serrato and Xu (2021).
  2. Externalities: why innovation creates them, and a map of the many kinds.
  3. König, Storesletten, Song and Zilibotti (2022): where is all that Chinese R&D going? Imitation, innovation and wedges, in a model estimated on Chinese firms.
  4. Banares-Sanchez, Burgess, László, Simpson, Van Reenen and Wang (2026), "Ray of Hope?", told in the seven steps: the cities' solar subsidies, a twin for each city (synthetic difference in differences), and a model to ask whether China gained. The second half is on Wednesday.
