Lecture 10 — Innovation: copy, invent, or relabel?

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Monday 23 November 2026, online on Teams · Block: innovation

Questions

  • How much does China spend on research and development, who spends it, and on what kind of research?
  • China files more patents than any other country. Are they good patents?
  • How does innovation make an economy grow, and why do old firms lose when new ones climb?
  • Should a country that is catching up copy or invent, and when should it switch?
  • Does a tax cut for R&D make firms do more research, or only report more?

Key concepts

R&D intensity · basic research, applied research, experimental development · patents, utility models, PCT applications · the quality ladder · creative destruction · distance to the frontier · imitation and innovation · selection · the trap · notches · regression discontinuity · bunching · relabelling.

In class

  1. The end of lecture 9: who paid in America? The China shock, and the homework.
  2. China's R&D: how much, who spends it, what kind; patents, and how good they are.
  3. Growth by creative destruction: the quality ladder, with the Ladder Lab next to the slides.
  4. Imitate or innovate? The model of Acemoglu, Aghion and Zilibotti, in words and in the Lab.
  5. Chen, Liu, Suárez Serrato and Xu (2021), told in the seven steps: a line in the tax code, two methods (regression discontinuity and bunching), and how much of the extra R&D is real.
  6. Next time.

Readings

On AulaWeb, enrolled students find the papers listed here, one zip file per lecture. Other books and a few older papers are not there, and some papers are there in their working-paper version.

Required

  • Zhao Chen, Zhikuo Liu, Juan Carlos Suárez Serrato and Daniel Yi Xu, "Notching R&D Investment with Corporate Income Tax Cuts in China", American Economic Review 111(7), 2021, pp. 2065-2100 — the introduction, Section I (the programme) and Section II (the evidence of bunching and relabelling), pp. 2065-2080.

Optional

  • Philippe Aghion and Peter Howitt, "A Model of Growth through Creative Destruction", Econometrica 60(2), 1992, pp. 323-351: the quality ladder.
  • Daron Acemoglu, Philippe Aghion and Fabrizio Zilibotti, "Distance to Frontier, Selection, and Economic Growth", Journal of the European Economic Association 4(1), 2006, pp. 37-74: imitate or innovate.
  • Fabrizio Zilibotti, "Growing and Slowing Down Like China", Journal of the European Economic Association 15(5), 2017, pp. 943-988: the same model, applied to China.
