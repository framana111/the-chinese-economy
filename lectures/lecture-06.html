Lecture 6 — Inside the sectors: state firms and misallocation

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Tuesday 10 November 2026 · Block: reallocation within sectors

Questions

  • What does an economy leaving the plan do with its state firms?
  • How much does it cost to keep capital and workers in the wrong firms?
  • What is the difference between TFPQ and TFPR, and why does a wedge show up in one and not in the other?
  • How much higher would manufacturing TFP be in China and India if capital and labour were allocated as well as in the United States?
  • What if the data are wrong?

Key concepts

Wedges on capital and labour · marginal revenue products (MRPL, MRPK) · TFPQ and TFPR · demand with a constant elasticity (σ) and the markup · misallocation · rules of the world · the US benchmark · errors in the data · a level, not a growth rate · "grasp the large, let go of the small".

In class

  1. The end of lecture 5: Khanna, Liang, Mobarak and Song (2025), and "gone or blocked?".
  2. Inside the sectors: the state firms, from 1978 to 2005.
  3. A model with many firms: two inputs, two wedges, two TFPs, and its rules of the world.
  4. The SOE Lab: three questions, on the screen.
  5. Hsieh and Klenow (2009), told in the seven steps.

Readings

On AulaWeb, enrolled students find the papers listed here, one zip file per lecture. Other books and a few older papers are not there, and some papers are there in their working-paper version.

Required

  • Chang-Tai Hsieh and Peter J. Klenow, "Misallocation and Manufacturing TFP in China and India", Quarterly Journal of Economics 124(4), 2009, pp. 1403-1448 — the introduction, the model (skip the derivations), and the results on the gains from reallocation and on the state-owned plants.

Optional (works cited on the slides)

  • Chang-Tai Hsieh and Zheng (Michael) Song, "Grasp the Large, Let Go of the Small: The Transformation of the State Sector in China", Brookings Papers on Economic Activity, Spring 2015, pp. 295-366, with the comments by Loren Brandt and Amit Khandelwal. We read it tomorrow.
  • Barry Naughton, The Chinese Economy: Transitions and Growth (MIT Press, 2007), chapter 8 (the layoffs) and chapter 13 (the state's share of industrial output).
  • Nicholas R. Lardy, Markets over Mao: The Rise of Private Business in China (Peterson Institute for International Economics, 2014): the state firms losing money in 1998.
  • Robert C. Feenstra, Robert Inklaar and Marcel P. Timmer, "The Next Generation of the Penn World Table", American Economic Review 105(10), 2015, pp. 3150-3182. The figure on the slides uses version 10.01 of the data, 2019.
  • Trevor Tombe and Xiaodong Zhu (2019) and Khanna, Liang, Mobarak and Song (2025): see lecture 5.

The SOE Lab

Open the SOE Lab: twelve state firms and thirty-six private firms share the same capital and workers. Move the wedges, press "Reallocate", upgrade the state firms, and add errors to the data.

For tomorrow

Think of the economy of the country where you grew up: Italy, or any other.

  1. Name at least three wedges in it: anything that makes some firms pay less, or more, for capital or workers, or keeps them from growing or shrinking.
  2. For each one: who gains, who loses, and what it costs the economy.
  3. Is it being removed, or getting stronger?

The SOE Lab can help you think. Write your answers down: I will ask you tomorrow, in class.
