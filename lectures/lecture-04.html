Lecture 4 — From the farm to the factory: the hukou

Slides (online)Slides (PDF)

Tuesday 3 November 2026 · Block: reallocation across sectors

Questions

  • What did the household registration system (hukou) do to labour mobility before 1979?
  • How can moving workers from the farm to the city raise total output, with no new technology?
  • What does a cost of moving do to where people work, to wages and to output? And who gains from it?

Key concepts

Household registration: category (agricultural or not) and place · changing status by the state's decision (nongzhuanfei) · production function and marginal product · Lewis's two economies and the turning point · free mobility between two sectors · a cost of moving, τ · output lost · a wage gap is not a welfare gap.

In class

  1. Your answers to last week's questions: Almond, Li and Zhang (2019); Chen, Fan, Gu and Zhou (2020).
  2. The hukou before 1979: the booklet, cheap grain for the cities, and who could change status.
  3. Two sectors: Lewis's picture, one more worker on the same land, and what happens when moving costs τ.
  4. The Hukou Wedge Lab: raise and lower the cost of moving and watch workers, wages and output.
  5. Next time: τ is a number, and it fell.

Readings

Required

  • Barry Naughton, The Chinese Economy: Transitions and Growth (MIT Press, 2007), chapter 5, "The Urban-Rural Divide" (pp. 113-136), with attention to the passages on household registration (pp. 115-116). Free to borrow (one-hour or two-week loan, free account) on the Internet Archive; the chapters required for this course will soon also be available to enrolled students on AulaWeb.
  • Kam Wing Chan and Will Buckingham, "Is China Abolishing the Hukou System?", China Quarterly 195, 2008, pp. 582-591 (the system and how changing status worked).

Optional (works cited on the slides)

  • W. Arthur Lewis, "Economic Development with Unlimited Supplies of Labour", The Manchester School 22(2), 1954, pp. 139-191.
  • Tiejun Cheng and Mark Selden, "The Origins and Social Consequences of China's Hukou System", China Quarterly 139, 1994, pp. 644-668.
  • Trevor Tombe and Xiaodong Zhu, "Trade, Migration, and Productivity: A Quantitative Analysis of China", American Economic Review 109(5), 2019, pp. 1843-1872 (the subject of lecture 5).
  • Robert E. Lucas, "Life Earnings and Rural-Urban Migration", Journal of Political Economy 112(S1), 2004, pp. S29-S59 (a dynamic model of migration, advanced).
